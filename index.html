Listen freely, transmit legally. Anyone may monitor these channels. Transmitting needs the right authorization: an FCC amateur license for ham bands (General class or higher for HF nets and 60 m), a GMRS license for GMRS, an agency license for public-safety channels. FRS, MURS and CB need no license. In a true emergency involving immediate risk to life, FCC rules allow any station to call for help on any frequency. Always confirm local ARES/RACES frequencies with your county emergency coordinator.

What Bad Day Rescue is for

Bad Day Rescue helps when something goes wrong. The power or internet is out at home and you need to know what is happening. You are traveling and need a hospital, or a 24-hour vet for your pet, in a town you don't know. A storm is coming and you need a shelter or a weather broadcast.

Everything in the app is stored inside one file. Once that file is on your phone or computer, the frequencies, hospitals, vets and shelter list all work with no internet and no cell data.

If you have a bad day

Internet or power is out at home

  1. Open the Frequencies tab and tap your FEMA region.
  2. Tap Broadcast & weather.
  3. Tune a battery or car radio to your area's AM station in the list. These stations carry national emergency alerts and stay on the air during disasters.
  4. For weather warnings, set a weather radio to one of the seven NOAA Weather Radio channels (162.400 to 162.550 MHz). Try each one until you hear your local forecast.

Traveling and need a hospital

  1. Call 911 first for anything life-threatening.
  2. Open Hospitals and type the town, county or ZIP code into the search box.
  3. Keep Emergency room only on so you only see hospitals with an ER.
  4. Call the number shown, or give the address to your map app.

Your pet is hurt or sick

  1. Open Emergency vets and search for the city or state you're in.
  2. Look for the 24/7 badge, and call before you drive so they can get ready.
  3. If your pet ate something toxic, call ASPCA Animal Poison Control at (888) 426-4435.

You need a shelter

  1. Text SHELTER and your ZIP code to 43362. A plain text message often gets through when calls and data do not.
  2. Open Shelters to see the shelters that were open when the app was last updated.
  3. Listen to your area's AM station for new shelter openings.

You have a two-way radio

  1. FRS radios (family walkie-talkies) need no license. Channel 20 is widely monitored for emergencies, and channel 1 is a common calling channel.
  2. CB channel 9 is the CB emergency channel.
  3. Ham operators: the Amateur section lists calling frequencies and emergency nets. You need a license to transmit, except to call for help when a life is in danger.

How to use the app

Tabs
Switch between Frequencies, Shelters, Hospitals, Emergency vets and Gear at the top of the screen. Gear lists what to buy before a bad day.
FEMA region
Tap a region card or pick one from the list to see only that area. Tap the same card again to see everything. Not sure of your region? Each card lists its states.
Search
Type a town, county, state, ZIP code, station name or frequency. Searches work on every tab.
Categories
On Frequencies, tap a category such as Broadcast & weather or Amateur to narrow the list.
★ Save
Tap the star next to a frequency to save it, then tap Saved only to see just your favorites. Saved items stay on that device and in that browser.
⧉ Copy
Copies a frequency so you can paste it into a message or note.

Get it ready before you need it

  • Add it to your home screen. Go to baddayrescue.com. On iPhone, open it in Safari, tap the Share button, then Add to Home Screen. On Android, open it in Chrome, tap the menu, then Add to Home screen or Install app.
  • Open it once while you still have service. After that first visit it is saved on your phone and works with no internet. It updates itself the next time you're online.
  • On a computer, you can also keep the file Bad Day Rescue.html and open it in any web browser.
  • Find your region now. Tap your region and star the AM station and NOAA channel for your home so they are one tap away.
  • Keep a battery-powered or hand-crank radio with AM, FM and NOAA weather bands. A phone can't receive these broadcasts on its own.
  • Write down the big numbers in case your phone dies: 911, Poison Control 1-800-222-1222, ASPCA Animal Poison Control (888) 426-4435, Red Cross 1-800-733-2767, and 211 for local help.
